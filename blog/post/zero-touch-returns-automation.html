How we built a zero-touch RMA system for a $20M DTC brand

Returns are one of the least glamorous parts of running a DTC brand — and one of the most operationally expensive if handled manually. A client processing 400+ returns a month had two people doing nothing but reviewing return requests, issuing labels, and updating spreadsheets by hand. We rebuilt it as a fully automated system.

Why manual RMA processing doesn't scale

Every return request needed a human to check the order, verify the return window, decide the refund amount (accounting for promo codes and partial returns), generate a shipping label, and update three separate systems — Shopify, the 3PL, and the accounting tool. Multiply that by 400 a month and it's a full-time job for two people that adds zero value to the business.

Step 1: Self-service intake with built-in rules

We built a return request form that checks eligibility automatically — order date against the return window, item condition flags, and whether the item is final-sale — before a request is even submitted. Roughly 60% of requests are now auto-approved instantly with zero human review.

💡
Most returns are simple. The 60% that follow standard rules don't need a human at all — save your team's attention for the 40% that are genuinely edge cases.

Step 2: Automatic label generation and routing

Once approved, a return label is generated automatically through the 3PL's API and emailed to the customer immediately — no waiting for a support agent to notice the request in a queue.

01

Edge cases still route to a human

Anything outside the standard rules — high-value items, repeat returners, or items flagged as damaged — automatically routes to a human reviewer with full context attached, instead of being auto-approved or auto-rejected blindly.

Step 3: Refunds and inventory, synced automatically

When the 3PL scans the returned item back into the warehouse, that event triggers the refund in Shopify and the accounting entry in QuickBooks simultaneously — the exact three-system update that used to take a person 10-15 minutes per return, now happens in seconds with no manual entry.

The results

  • 60% of returns now fully auto-approved and processed with zero human touch
  • Average return-to-refund time dropped from 6 days to under 24 hours
  • The two team members previously doing manual RMA processing were redeployed to customer experience work

"Our customers noticed the speed before we even announced anything. Faster refunds turned out to be a retention lever we didn't know we had."
